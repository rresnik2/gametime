A · Ringward

37 / 40 · recommendedPortrait 1080 × 19202:00 roundsSolo, duo, raids

Keep the core alive for two minutes. Shards fly at it from every side, and on a stream the viewers are the ones throwing them (decision 010). Drawn to docs/game/design.md on the game PR (rresnik2/gametime#8): score above the arena, time left inside the core, three crack pips below it, raider names on dark pills inside the rim, P1 purple and P2 near-white shields, overdrive in the last 20 s.

In the feed, and the game screen behind it

R1The stream in the feed · raid in progressFor You feed, portrait stream (decision 011)
  1. Swipe-in partly passes: the mint core, its countdown, the purple arc and the score read at once. But the one incoming shard is behind the Raid button (only its tip shows) and the next launch's warning is under chat, so the threat itself is mostly hidden until a shard crosses the clear middle. Compare R1b. A crack is rarer still (this streamer: one in 52 s).
  2. Raid button (Ringward streams only, and only while a round is on; between rounds the rail is the usual four buttons) opens the Raid sheet R8. Five buttons push the rail up to about 350 pt, over the arena's right edge.
  3. Overlay collision: raider names sit under the overlay: pixelpilot's pill is cut by the Raid button, and mintmoth's rim warning (the next launch, from 7 o'clock) is under chat. Shards launch from every side, and 6 of the 12 slots (3 to 8 o'clock) start under chat or the rail.
  4. Overlay collision: the crack pips sit between the last chat line and the streamer's name, under the darkest part of the scrim, and "RAID ON · 12 raiding" is hidden behind the name row (its tail peeks out after the game chip, where it reads as app UI). Cracks are the stakes; they shouldn't be the hardest thing to see.
  5. The fix is a smaller arena: see R1b.
R2Game screen · mid-round, raid onOBS canvas 1080 × 1920
  1. Score, huge and centred.
  2. Multiplier: ×4 (one step per 8 combo, max ×5).
  3. Rim warning: the launch slot flashes red 0.6 s before the shard flies, so viewers see the threat a beat before the player reacts. The raider's name (mintmoth) shows on the warning too: the greybox already draws it, but design.md doesn't say so yet.

  5. Raider names ride their shards on dark pills, inside the rim. Raid shards always come from one of the 12 rim slots (every 30°, the ticks).
  6. Crack pips: two left. The third crack ends the round.
  7. One-line status (decision 012's proposal): "RAID ON · 12 raiding". In solo with no viewers it reads "SOLO · best 7,960". At 1:08 the wave table gives a shard every 1.0 s that takes 1.35 s to reach the core, so a viewer usually sees one shard nearing the shield, the last one bouncing away, and the next one's warning.
A newcomer's first 5 seconds

0–1 sA dark screen, one bright mint circle with "1:08" counting down, a purple arc around it, a red shard closing in. "2,900" on top.

1–3 sA shard bounces off the arc with a flash (+40, or "PERFECT +100" off the middle, about one block in four). A rim slot flashes red with a name on it, and a shard follows.

3–5 sUsually another block. Rarely (this streamer: once in 52 s) one gets past, the core cracks and a pip goes dark; the empty pip already says that has happened. Names on the shards say viewers are throwing them, so the next thing they want is the Raid button.

In the feed (R1) much less of this reads: the incoming shard is behind the Raid button, mintmoth's warning is under chat, and the pips are under the scrim. The core, arc, countdown and score still say "defend the circle", but the threat, the names and the stakes mostly don't show until a shard crosses the clear middle.

R1bProposal: the same moment, laid out for the feedFor You feed · overlay-safe layout
  1. Arena shrinks to a 340 px radius centred at y 800 (63% of the width, down from 91%), so its whole rim, every launch slot included, is above chat and left of the Raid-height rail. Its lower half is still under the top of the scrim (about 25% darker at the rim), which dims but doesn't hide it.
  2. One top row: multiplier, score, and the three crack pips beside it (90% of R2's size), all in the clear band. "RAID ON · 12 raiding" sits between the score and the arena.
  3. Same timing, smaller motion. The game runs in arena units, so rim to core still takes 1.35 s, but shards travel about 30% fewer pixels and read smaller. Names stay readable (40 px on the canvas, about 14 pt here), but fixed-size pills crowd a smaller ring sooner.
  4. Trade-off for the team: R1 is the more striking game on a monitor; R1b is the one that reads in the feed. Draft only. If Ringward is picked, its design doc would adopt one of the two, and the lower half of the canvas could hold something viewers can lose (best score, the raider leaderboard on the result card).

C · Hot Core

29 / 40 · fallbackPortrait 1080 × 19201:30 rounds (assumed)Viewers vote bank / hold

A push-your-luck game on one button. Hold to pump energy into a reactor; the points at risk grow faster the longer you hold, but once it's in the danger zone it can overload at any moment, and the only warning is a flicker in its glow that gets harder to read each level. Let go to bank. The concept has no design doc, so these mockups make five assumptions: 1:30 rounds (from the first concept draft; decision 012 allows any length), levels that speed up the charge and come every 15 s, a charge gauge that fills while you hold (overload becomes possible once it enters its red last third and likelier as it fills; a full gauge always overloads; the reactor glows red while it's in that zone), a flicker that appears only in the last second or so before an overload (fainter each level, so a player who spots it can still let go in time), and a viewer bank / hold vote from the app as its viewer interaction (scored 2 of 5 in concepts.md, because stream delay makes votes late). Unlike Ringward, it was laid out from the start to keep everything above the feed's overlay.

In the feed, and the game screen behind it

H1The stream in the feed · mid-roundFor You feed, portrait stream
  1. What matters is above the overlay: level, timer, banked score, the vote, the reactor and the points at risk. The bottom of the gauge touches the first chat line. Nothing else sits under chat: a "last banks" history was tried there and made chat harder to read, so it lives on the end card (H4) instead.
  2. The timing is invisible at this size. How much is at stake reads fine ("+1,350 AT RISK", the vote, the gauge). But the flicker (the white flecks round the core) is the game's only warning of when it will overload, and here it's a few pixels that compression may erase. Viewers can't feel the moment the player is gambling on.
  3. Viewer vote. Needs an app control (a rail button like Ringward's Raid, not drawn) and arrives a few seconds late, so it's a crowd mood rather than a real call.
H2Game screen · mid-round, holdingOBS canvas 1080 × 1920 · an earlier round than H4/H5
  1. Level: each level charges faster and makes the flicker harder to read.
  2. Round timer.
  3. Banked: safe points. This is the score.
  4. Viewer vote, bank vs hold, live from the app.
  5. Charge gauge: 24 segments, here just into red. In the red last third an overload can happen at any moment and gets likelier; full always overloads. So it shows the danger zone, not the moment. The flicker only shows in the last second or so before an overload.
  6. At risk: what you lose if it overloads now, inside the reactor. "HOLDING 3.2 s" above it.
  7. The flicker: the faint white fleck at about 1 o'clock on the core's edge (another at 7) is the only overload warning, drawn at its real size: here it means this hold blows within about a second unless the player lets go. The player watching for it can react; you may need to zoom to find it, and after video compression it is likely gone.
A newcomer's first 5 seconds

0–1 sOne big glowing reactor, red, its ring of segments just into red, a faint flicker already on, "+1,350 AT RISK" climbing inside it, "4,820 BANKED" on top, and the vote bar leaning to BANK. Within about a second the player either lets go (the number flies up into BANKED) or misses the flicker and the reactor overloads in a white flash.

1–5 sThe next hold starts from an empty ring and the number climbs again; by now the newcomer gets it. One who swipes in between holds sees only a number and an empty ring until the next hold starts.

In the feed (H1) all of this reads, except the flicker, and chat is shouting "BANK IT".

B · Lumen Drop

28 / 40Portrait 1080 × 1920Endless run, ends on redViewers place gates

Steer a falling spark down an endless vertical shaft. Gates rise towards it in pairs, one mint and one red: pass through mint to grow the combo, touch red and the run ends. Every ten gates the shaft narrows and speeds up. Viewers place gate pairs ahead of the spark from the app (concepts.md: 3 of 5; every gate must stay fair). Assumptions drawn here: a pair is a mint gate and a red bar with open space around them, and flying past a mint gate without going through it resets the combo. The score is distance × the current combo, as concepts.md says, so a combo reset would cut it sharply; whether the score should instead build up is an open question for the design doc. Red bars carry a dark hatch so they don't rely on colour alone.

What all three share

  • Portrait games fill the phone, so the overlay covers their lower 40%. On the 1080 × 1920 canvas, the clear band runs from about y 280 (below the LIVE pill) to y 1140 (chat starts at y 1166; this keeps a small margin). Below that: chat on the left, the action rail on the right from y 1150 (y 920 with Ringward's Raid button), streamer info and the chat input, all under a scrim that darkens from y 800 down. Turn on Show app overlay to see it on every canvas.
  • Reference device: these frames assume a 375 × 812 pt phone with the 9:16 stream fitted to the width (about 31 pt bands above and below it), as decision 011's "nothing is cropped" implies. On a shorter phone (16:9-class, like an SE) or with aspect-fill, the scale and the clear band shift; re-check the band on the shortest phone the app supports.
  • Proposed rule for the chosen game's design doc: keep the score, the goal or status line and the main action inside y 280–1140, and don't put anything a newcomer must read in the lower left or along the right edge below y 900.
  • Clean view: per wireframe W1, a single tap hides the whole overlay. That helps a viewer who has decided to watch, but the swipe-in impression comes before any tap, so the collisions above still count.
  • Text sizes: the canvas is shown at about 35% in the feed, so 40 px on the canvas is about 14 pt on a phone. Nothing a viewer needs is drawn under 40 px here.
  • Each game shows a one-line status (Ringward "RAID ON · 12 raiding", Hot Core "LEVEL 3", Lumen Drop "SHAFT 4" with 6 of 10 blocks lit), the persistent line decision 012 proposes for swipe-in.
  • Look-alikes: at thumbnail size Ringward and Hot Core look similar (a glowing disc, Hot Core's mint until its gauge reaches red, in a dark segmented ring under a big white number). If both stay in contention, give Hot Core its own silhouette.
  • Not drawn: two-player modes for Hot Core (a race) and Lumen Drop (two sparks), which concepts.md scores low; sound; motion. A real stream also adds video compression, which these flat shapes survive better than thin lines.
