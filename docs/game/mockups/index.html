A · Ringward (working title): fish in a barrel

Picked · decision 009, Nov 17, 202637 / 40Portrait 1080 × 19202:00 roundsSolo, duo, raids

Shooting fish in a barrel, except the fish fights back. A round orange goldfish with red lips swings a shield on a stick to bat away cartoon cannonballs fired in from every side, for two minutes; on a stream, the viewers fire them (decision 010). Every ball that gets through is a "BONK!" that pops one of three bubbles and leaves slapstick injuries that pile up: bruises, band-aids, a black eye, and finally a dizzy spiral eye. Drawn to docs/game/design.md on PR rresnik2/gametime#108 (fish-in-a-barrel theme, not merged yet): time, score and multiplier above the barrel, the bubbles below it, thrower names on cream-on-dark pills inside the rim, and the 12 viewer slots on the stave joints. The game has its own palette and an old-film finish (sepia, grain, vignette, a scratch or two) with slab-serif lettering (Rockwell where installed, Georgia otherwise, as in the greybox). GameTime's tokens apply only to the app overlay.

Name: "Ringward" is the working title and is what the frames show. Decision 009 proposes Finvincible, Goldie's Barrel and Bonk Barrel (each needs a web and app-store search first). Renaming changes the title card (R3), the game chip in the feed and the Raid sheet's heading.

Originality: generic vintage-film and carnival-postcard cues, no stripes, clownfish or named characters. The one thing to watch in Phase 3 art is the 1930s-cartoon-plus-film-grain combination: keep away from pie-cut eyes, rubber-hose limbs and that era's title-card styling.

A newcomer's first 5 seconds

0–1 sA sepia barrel of lagoon water, a round orange goldfish with red lips and a crossed band-aid over a bruise, a purple shield on a stick, a cannonball trailing smoke. "2,900" on top, "1:08" beside it.

1–3 sThe ball clangs off the shield (+40, or "PERFECT +100" off the middle, about one block in four). A red ring flashes on the rim with a name on it, and a ball follows.

3–5 sUsually another block. Rarely (this streamer: once in 52 s) one gets through: "BONK!", the fish squashes, a bubble pops and it gets a black eye and a second band-aid. The first band-aid already tells the story. Names on the balls say viewers are firing them, so the next thing they want is the Raid button.

In the feed (R1) the barrel, the fish, its injuries, the score and the time all read; the incoming ball, mintmoth's warning and the bubbles are under the overlay.

  2. One top row: time, score and the three bubbles, all in the clear band; "×4 · 12 throwing" between the score and the barrel.
  3. The trade-off: the game runs in arena units, so the timing is the same, but everything is drawn about 30% smaller. The fish is about 55 pt wide here (80 pt in R1), so its bruises and band-aids are harder to read, and the fixed-size name pills crowd a smaller ring sooner.
  4. R1 is the more striking game on a monitor; R1b is the one that reads in the feed. Draft only, for the team to pick before Phase 3 art.

C · Hot Core · not picked (decision 009)

29 / 40 · not pickedPortrait 1080 × 19201:30 rounds (assumed)Viewers vote bank / hold

A push-your-luck game on one button. Hold to pump energy into a reactor; the points at risk grow faster the longer you hold, but once it's in the danger zone it can overload at any moment, and the only warning is a flicker in its glow that gets harder to read each level. Let go to bank. The concept has no design doc, so these mockups make five assumptions: 1:30 rounds (from the first concept draft; decision 012 allows any length), levels that speed up the charge and come every 15 s, a charge gauge that fills while you hold (overload becomes possible once it enters its red last third and likelier as it fills; a full gauge always overloads; the reactor glows red while it's in that zone), a flicker that appears only in the last second or so before an overload (fainter each level, so a player who spots it can still let go in time), and a viewer bank / hold vote from the app as its viewer interaction (scored 2 of 5 in concepts.md, because stream delay makes votes late). Unlike Ringward, it was laid out from the start to keep everything above the feed's overlay.

B · Lumen Drop · not picked (decision 009)

28 / 40 · not pickedPortrait 1080 × 1920Endless run, ends on redViewers place gates

Steer a falling spark down an endless vertical shaft. Gates rise towards it in pairs, one mint and one red: pass through mint to grow the combo, touch red and the run ends. Every ten gates the shaft narrows and speeds up. Viewers place gate pairs ahead of the spark from the app (concepts.md: 3 of 5; every gate must stay fair). Assumptions drawn here: a pair is a mint gate and a red bar with open space around them, and flying past a mint gate without going through it resets the combo. The score is distance × the current combo, as concepts.md says, so a combo reset would cut it sharply; whether the score should instead build up is an open question for the design doc. Red bars carry a dark hatch so they don't rely on colour alone.

What all three share

  • Portrait games fill the phone, so the overlay covers their lower 40%. On the 1080 × 1920 canvas, the clear band runs from about y 280 (below the LIVE pill) to y 1140 (chat starts at y 1166; this keeps a small margin). Below that: chat on the left, the action rail on the right from y 1150 (y 920 with Ringward's Raid button), streamer info and the chat input, all under a scrim that darkens from y 800 down. Turn on Show app overlay to see it on every canvas.
  • Reference device: these frames assume a 375 × 812 pt phone with the 9:16 stream fitted to the width (about 31 pt bands above and below it), as decision 011's "nothing is cropped" implies. On a shorter phone (16:9-class, like an SE) or with aspect-fill, the scale and the clear band shift; re-check the band on the shortest phone the app supports.
  • Proposed rule for the chosen game's design doc: keep the score, the goal or status line and the main action inside y 280–1140, and don't put anything a newcomer must read in the lower left or along the right edge below y 900.
  • Clean view: per wireframe W1, a single tap hides the whole overlay. That helps a viewer who has decided to watch, but the swipe-in impression comes before any tap, so the collisions above still count.
  • Text sizes: the canvas is shown at about 35% in the feed, so 40 px on the canvas is about 14 pt on a phone. Nothing a viewer needs is drawn under 40 px here.
  • Each game shows a one-line status (Ringward "12 throwing" during a raid, with no status line in a solo round yet, Hot Core "LEVEL 3", Lumen Drop "SHAFT 4" with 6 of 10 blocks lit), the persistent line decision 012 proposes for swipe-in.
  • Look-alikes: before the re-theme, Ringward and Hot Core looked alike at thumbnail size (a glowing disc in a dark ring under a big number). The barrel, the fish and the sepia finish settle that.
  • Not drawn: two-player modes for Hot Core (a race) and Lumen Drop (two sparks), which concepts.md scores low; sound; motion. A real stream also adds video compression, which these flat shapes survive better than thin lines.
